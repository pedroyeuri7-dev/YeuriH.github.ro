01 | Flagship system

RetailCast

An integrated demand forecasting and inventory optimization system built to solve the core problem in retail planning: making reliable allocation decisions with incomplete demand visibility.

Supply Chain | Demand Forecasting | Inventory Optimization

RetailCast

The fundamental challenge in retail operations: planning teams make stock allocation decisions with unreliable demand signals. The result is a choice between carrying excess inventory (tying up capital, creating markdowns) or running with insufficient stock (losing sales, frustrating customers). RetailCast was built to improve that signal.

Business problem
Store teams lack forward visibility into demand patterns. Allocation decisions rely on manual forecasts or simple rules. The cost: either overstock (capital waste) or stockouts (lost revenue).
Technical approach
Multi-method ensemble combining ARIMA, Prophet, LightGBM, and LSTM. System evaluates each method against actual sales, selecting the approach that minimizes prediction error for each product family.
Supply chain benefit
Planners get weekly demand forecasts by product and location. Actual accuracy: 84% improvement over naive baseline. Actionable output: optimized allocation guidance and safety stock calculations.

Why ensemble over single model

Retail demand exhibits multiple patterns: trends (seasonal uplift), autocorrelation (momentum), external factors (weather, promotions). No single algorithm captures all. The ensemble approach tests ARIMA for trend, Prophet for seasonality, LightGBM for feature interactions, and LSTM for sequence patterns. Weighted voting selects the best for each product family.

Forecast accuracy validation

Accuracy measured on held-out 42-day period across 54 store locations and 33 product families. RMSE reduced from 1,361 (naive) to 215 (model). This translates to: allocation error reduced by 84%. Measured impact on inventory turns and markdown rate.

How planners use it

Interactive dashboard surfaces forecasts by week, product, and location. Planners compare allocation scenarios, see demand spike probability, and receive reorder point recommendations. System flags high-variance products requiring manual review. Integration with ERP for automatic replenishment triggers.

Implementation considerations

Forecast accuracy varies by product velocity (high-movers are easier to predict than slow movers). System includes confidence intervals for each forecast. Safety stock calculated using z-score method (service level 95%). Reorder points account for lead time variability.

02 | Customer analytics system

ClientIQ

A customer segmentation and churn prediction platform that helps small and mid-market retailers identify high-value customers, flag churn risk, and execute retention strategies. Designed for supply chain teams managing SKU allocation by customer value.

Customer Analytics | RFM Segmentation | Churn Modeling

ClientIQ

In retail, allocation decisions are often product-centric. But the real question is customer-centric: which customers represent 80% of our profit? Who's at risk of leaving? Where should we allocate premium inventory to maximize revenue per customer? ClientIQ answers these by building a complete customer intelligence layer on transaction history.

Supply chain context
Buyers lack visibility into customer behavior. Allocation is driven by historical sales velocity, not customer value trajectory. Result: premium inventory distributed evenly instead of to high-value, high-retention customers.
Analytical solution
RFM segmentation (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) combined with churn risk scoring. Machine learning model predicts 60-day return probability for each customer. Segments customers into tiers with specific retention actions.
Supply chain impact
Buyers can now allocate stock by customer segment, not just product. High-value, at-risk customers get priority allocation. Low-value customers see slower replenishment. Result: improved customer retention, revenue per transaction, and inventory turns.

RFM customer segmentation

Three signals: Recency (days since purchase), Frequency (total transactions), Monetary (total spend). System scores each customer 0-999 on RFM composite. Clusters emerge: top spenders (allocate premium), regulars (maintain), at-risk (retention campaign), lost (reactivation). Each segment gets specific allocation and marketing strategy.

Churn prediction modeling

Random Forest classifier trained on customer behavior patterns. Predicts: will this customer purchase in next 60 days? Features: days since last purchase, purchase frequency trend, seasonality patterns, value volatility. Output: churn risk score 0-100. Threshold determines when to trigger retention action.

Allocation strategy by segment

Champions (high value, loyal): first access to new products, premium inventory reserves. At-risk (high value, declining): targeted promotions, exclusive allocations. At-potential (low value now, growth trajectory): deliberate building of inventory for upside capture. Lost (declining spend): clearance allocation, reactivation offers.

Retention execution

System produces weekly priority contact list ranked by customer lifetime value. Integration with CRM for automated outreach (email, SMS). Tracks win-back effectiveness. Dashboards show cohort retention rates by segment. Feedback loop: allocation decisions informed by customer response data.

03 | How I solve problems

Analytical methodology

A systematic approach to supply chain and planning problems, informed by domain experience and technical capability.

01 | Define the constraint

Every supply chain problem has a binding constraint: capacity, demand visibility, cost structure, service level. I start by identifying it. Is allocation failing because of poor demand signal, or because of inventory policy? This determines the solution direction.

02 | Map the decision flow

Who makes this decision? What information do they currently use? What's missing? I trace the decision from data source through execution, identifying where breakdown occurs. This shows whether the problem is analytical or operational.

03 | Engineer the data layer

Clean source systems, validate data quality, engineer features. For forecasting: lags, rolling stats, seasonality decomposition. For segmentation: RFM, customer health metrics. Garbage in → garbage out. Data engineering is 70% of the work.

04 | Build the model

Select algorithm based on problem structure, not on what's trendy. Demand forecasting: ensemble. Churn: classification. Optimization: linear programming. I validate on held-out test data, measure performance in business terms (not just statistical metrics).

05 | Operationalize and integrate

Model sits in development until it changes decisions. I build for integration: API endpoints, automated retraining, monitoring. Output goes into dashboards, emails, or ERP systems. Usability determines adoption.

06 | Measure and iterate

Track whether model decisions improved outcomes (revenue, turns, service level). Identify new constraints that emerged after implementing first solution. Supply chain is a system—optimization at one point can create problems elsewhere.
