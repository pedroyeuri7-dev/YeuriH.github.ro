RetailCast

Retailers regularly face the same two problems: too much stock in the wrong place, or not enough where it matters. Poor forecasting sits behind both. RetailCast was built to close that gap, turning sales history into clear, actionable demand signals that planners can actually use.

The problem
Store teams and planners were making replenishment decisions without reliable forward visibility into demand.
What was built
A forecasting system that combines sales history, seasonality, and product trends to generate weekly demand estimates by product family.
Who it helps
Demand planners, inventory analysts, and store operations teams who need practical visibility rather than a raw model output.

Forecasting approach

The system tests multiple forecasting methods against real retail data, including trend and seasonality models, to find which approach best fits different product types and sales patterns. Forecasts are evaluated on how close they come to actual sales, not just how complex they are.

Planning output

Results are presented through an interactive dashboard designed for non-technical users. Planners can review forecasts by product, compare scenarios, and spot where demand is likely to spike or dip before it happens.

ClientIQ

Most small businesses collect sales data but have no way to turn it into decisions. They don't know which customers are their most valuable, who hasn't come back in a while, or where to focus their time. ClientIQ was built to answer those questions from a simple sales file, no technical background required.

The problem
Small businesses lose customers without noticing. There's no easy way to see who's at risk, who's valuable, or who to contact first.
What was built
A dashboard that takes transaction data and automatically groups customers by behaviour, flags who is at risk of leaving, and produces a prioritised contact list.
The result
Business owners can see their most valuable customers, who to win back this week, and how their customer base is trending over time.

How it groups customers

The tool looks at three things for each customer: how recently they bought, how often they buy, and how much they spend. Using these signals, it automatically groups customers into tiers such as top spenders, regulars, occasional buyers, and those who are slipping away.

Predicting who will return

A prediction model runs on each customer to estimate whether they are likely to come back in the next 60 days. This turns a historical view of sales into a forward-looking tool, giving businesses a chance to act before a customer is lost.

Win-back and planning

The tool produces a prioritised list of customers to contact, sorted by estimated annual value. It also shows revenue trends by month and by day of the week, so businesses can spot their busiest periods and plan accordingly.

Built for different business types

The tool adapts to five types of business: retail stores, barbershops and salons, restaurants and cafes, service providers, and general retail. Each gets its own labels, thresholds, and suggested actions written in plain language.
